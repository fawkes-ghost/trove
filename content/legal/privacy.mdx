This notice explains what personal data Trove collects, why, who else handles it for us, how long we keep it and what you can ask us to do with it.

## Who we are

1. {t.promoter}. We decide how your personal data is used, which makes us the controller under UK data protection law.
2. Write to us about your data at <a href={`mailto:${t.contactEmail}`}>{t.contactEmail}</a>.

## What we collect today

1. **Your email address**, when you join the founding friends list, so we can tell you when entries open and send you news about Trove.
2. **How you arrived**: the campaign tags in the link you followed, the page that sent you and the first page you landed on. This stays in your browser until you join the list, and is sent with your email address only then. It tells us which posts and partners work.
3. **Your IP address**, recorded when you join the list. We use it only to spot automated sign-ups and fraud.
4. **How you heard about us**, if you choose to answer the one optional question after you confirm your place.
5. **Analytics**, only if you accept them in the banner. We use Google Analytics to count visits and see which pages people read. If you decline, no analytics tag loads.

## What we will collect when entries open

1. To enter a paid draw you will create an account with your first and last name, email address, mobile number, date of birth and postcode. We use your date of birth and postcode to check you are {t.minimumAge} or over and live in the UK, and your mobile number to reach you if you win.
2. We keep a record of your entries, your orders and any spending limits, breaks or closures you set, because the rules of a fair draw and the DCMS voluntary code require it.
3. Card details are taken by our payment provider. We never see or store your full card number.
4. If you enter by post, we keep the details on your card so your entry can be counted and checked.

## Why we are allowed to use it

1. **Your consent**: the founding friends list, marketing emails or texts, and analytics. You can withdraw consent at any time, and it is as easy as giving it.
2. **To carry out our agreement with you**: running the draw you entered, contacting the winner and awarding the prize.
3. **Legal obligations**: age and residency checks, keeping records of the draw and of entries, and the controls the DCMS voluntary code asks of us.
4. **Our legitimate interests**: keeping the site secure, stopping automated sign-ups and fraud, and understanding which channels bring people to Trove. We use only what is needed for each.

## Who else handles it

1. We use a small number of services that process data on our behalf, under written terms:
   - Supabase, which stores the founding friends list and, later, accounts and entries.
   - Vercel, which hosts this website.
   - Resend, which sends our emails.
   - Cloudflare Turnstile, which checks that a form is sent by a person, not a script.
   - Google Analytics, only if you accept analytics.
   - Our payment provider, once entries open.
2. Some of these services are based outside the UK, including in the United States. Where data leaves the UK it is protected by UK adequacy regulations or the UK’s approved contract terms.
3. We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with anyone for their own marketing.
4. If you win, we publish your first name and county on the result page, as the terms say. We share your details with the venue and travel providers only to arrange the escape.

## How long we keep it

1. The founding friends list: until you leave it, or two years after we last wrote to you, whichever comes first.
2. Accounts, entries and orders: for as long as your account is open and then six years, the period in which a claim about a draw could be brought.
3. Analytics: Google Analytics keeps event data for fourteen months.
4. If you close your account, we keep the minimum needed to honour the closure and to keep the draw records complete.

## Your rights

1. You can ask for a copy of your data, ask us to correct it, ask us to delete it, ask us to stop or limit using it, object to how we use it, and ask for it in a form you can take elsewhere.
2. Every marketing email has a one-click unsubscribe link.
3. Write to <a href={`mailto:${t.contactEmail}`}>{t.contactEmail}</a>. We answer within one month.
4. If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at <a href="https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/">ico.org.uk</a> or on 0303 123 1113. We would like the chance to put it right first.

## Changes

1. If we change this notice in a way that matters, we tell the people it affects by email before the change takes effect.
